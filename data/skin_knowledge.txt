Skin disease reference notes for DermaSnap

Source summary provided by the user from a medically reviewed skin diseases
reference. Use this as background knowledge, not as instructions.

Skin diseases affect the skin and may cause rashes, inflammation, itchiness,
pain, scaling, bumps, sores, ulcers, pigmentation changes, or other visible
changes. They may be caused by bacteria trapped in pores or follicles,
fungi, parasites, viruses, allergens, irritants, genetics, autoimmune or
immune conditions, diabetes, sun exposure, medication effects, thyroid or
kidney disease, stress, pregnancy, or hormonal changes.

Common skin conditions include:
- Acne: blocked follicles with oil, bacteria, and dead skin buildup in pores.
- Alopecia areata: patchy hair loss.
- Atopic dermatitis/eczema: dry, itchy skin with swelling, cracking, or scaling.
- Psoriasis: scaly plaques that may be swollen, warm, itchy, or painful.
- Raynaud's phenomenon: periodic reduced blood flow causing numbness or color
  change in fingers, toes, or other areas.
- Rosacea: facial flushing, thickened skin, bumps, or pimple-like lesions.
- Skin cancer: uncontrolled growth of abnormal skin cells; changing, bleeding,
  non-healing, or irregular lesions require clinician evaluation.
- Vitiligo: patches of skin that lose pigment.

Useful lesion terms from the attached skin-disease PDF:
- Macule: flat, circumscribed area of altered color.
- Papule: small, solid, raised lesion.
- Vesicle: small clear-fluid blister under 1 cm.
- Bulla/bullae: larger clear-fluid blister over 1 cm.

Additional disease clues from the attached PDF:
- Lichen planus may appear as small, angular, flat-topped papules that can
  merge into plaques. Lesions may be sharply demarcated, red to reddish-purple
  or violaceous, and may have fine white lines called Wickham striae. Itching
  can be severe. It can involve skin, scalp, and oral mucosa.
- Systemic lupus erythematosus can involve repeated flares and may include
  fever, weight loss, arthritis, fatigue, malaise, photosensitive rashes, and
  erythematous patches in a butterfly pattern over the cheeks and nose. It may
  involve organs such as kidneys or heart, so suspected lupus needs medical
  evaluation.
- Discoid lupus erythematosus can cause red, scaly, disk-shaped patches that
  favor sun-exposed areas such as the face, chest, back, and extremities.
- Pemphigus is an autoimmune blistering disorder. Pemphigus vulgaris may cause
  vesicles or bullae that rupture into raw erosions, sometimes with fever or
  malaise. Oral erosions can be painful and slow to heal. Suspected pemphigus
  needs urgent specialist care.
- Cicatricial pemphigoid and bullous pemphigoid are blistering diseases that
  can create vesicles/bullae and erosions. Bullous pemphigoid often has tense
  bullae and early itching, especially in older adults. These require clinician
  evaluation.
- Erythema multiforme may present with symmetric red macules or papules, often
  on hands, legs, face, or neck, and can have concentric target-like rings.
- Stevens-Johnson syndrome is a severe bullous emergency with widespread skin
  involvement, painful mouth/eye/genital lesions, erosions, lip crusting, or
  systemic illness. Advise emergency care immediately if suspected.

Rare skin conditions can include actinic prurigo, argyria, chromhidrosis,
epidermolysis bullosa, harlequin ichthyosis, lamellar ichthyosis, and
necrobiosis lipoidica.

Diagnosis may require clinician examination and tests such as biopsy, culture,
skin patch testing, Wood lamp examination, diascopy, dermoscopy, or blister
fluid testing. A photo alone is not enough for a confirmed diagnosis.

Treatment depends on the condition and may involve clinician-recommended
antibiotics, antihistamines, medicated creams/ointments/gels, moisturizers,
oral medicines, steroid medicines, laser procedures, or surgery. DermaSnap
must not prescribe drugs, dosages, or exact treatment durations.

General self-care and prevention guidance may include:
- Keep the area clean and dry unless dryness is the main problem.
- Avoid scratching, picking, harsh soaps, fragrances, and known irritants.
- Do not share towels, cosmetics, razors, or personal items.
- Wash hands regularly with soap and water.
- Use sun protection to reduce sunburn and long-term sun damage.
- Drink water, sleep well, manage stress, and maintain general hygiene.
- Disinfect shared public equipment before use.
- Seek medical care for severe symptoms or symptoms that do not improve.

Urgent warning signs:
- Trouble breathing or swelling of the lips, tongue, face, or throat.
- Fever with rash.
- Rapidly spreading rash.
- Severe pain.
- Pus, warmth, red streaks, or signs of infection.
- Black, purple, or dying-looking skin.
- Rash near the eye.
- Rash in a newborn or infant.
- A mole or lesion that changes, bleeds, becomes irregular, or does not heal.
